# Ultimate Editors — Short-Form Editing Brief (Remotion)

Learn the brief step by step and apply it to every short-form talking-head edit built in Remotion.

## How the brief is laid out (reading order)

1. **Captions and stylisation** (top left)
   - Two caption kinds: a **sans** font and a **script** font.
   - Font placement, colour and animations are each defined in the brief.
   - Use all of it for every caption.
2. **Caption inspiration board** (right of the captions section)
   - Examples of how captions are made for short-form. Use it as inspiration **every time** captions are created.
3. **Hook document** (under the captions)
   - Hook psychology: how the viewer is hooked into the video.
   - **Keep the speaker on screen** during the hook.
   - Show the right text combinations between sounds and script, and the right lines to keep viewers hooked.
   - Learn how viewers are kept hooked throughout the video, not only at the start.
4. **On-screen animations guide** (right of the hook document)
   - First the **three kinds of animations** that are created.
   - Then the **four categories**, each with a short description of how it is made:
     - text-centric
     - object-centric
     - character-centric
     - video-centric
   - Study the assets, effects, shapes, placement and construction of each. Use the images that represent each scene as inspiration **every time** an animation is created in Remotion.
5. **"How to Create Animations" guide** (under the animations guide)
   - How to build the background, put together the placement, add effects to the objects, and stylise the scene. Follow it to build high-quality animations in Remotion.
6. **Big inspiration board** (left of the animation guides)
   - UIs, graphs and many kinds of scenes. Always use it as a reference when creating an animation in Remotion.
7. **On-screen animations category** (bottom)
   - How on-screen elements ("on-screen eyes") are placed over the footage, with the **background darkened and blurred behind them**.
   - Use the sheet to the right of it as inspiration every time an on-screen animation is created.
